# Results

Every figure on this page is generated by `bench report` from a committed
run directory under [`results/`](../results/) and the committed SLO file
[`docs/slo.json`](slo.json). Generated text sits between
`bench-report:begin` and `bench-report:end` markers, and the first line of
each block records the exact command and the inputs it was made from. The
command rewrites only what is between the markers; the prose outside them is
hand-written and contains no measured numbers. Metric definitions,
percentile method and the goodput definitions are in
[02-architecture.md](02-architecture.md#metric-definitions-client-side-per-request).

## How to read the tables

- **c** is the closed-loop concurrency: that many requests in flight for the
  whole measured window, after a warmup that is excluded.
- **SLO cells** show the level's percentile and its margin against the
  limit, so a pass or a fail by a hair is visible as such. A value equal to
  the limit passes.
- **Max compliant goodput** (the headline) is the highest output tok/s
  among the levels that meet the SLO, have no errors, and pass the engine
  check. It answers "how much can this engine serve while every level's
  tail stays inside the budget".
- **Per-request goodput** counts only the output tokens of requests that
  met every limit on their own. It shows how much of a failing level's
  output was still useful, and it is never the headline.
- **Engine check**: the engine-side guard from the cross-check. A level
  whose prompts were served from the prefix cache instead of prefilled is
  marked `CACHED` and does not count.

The SLO was chosen after the baseline sweeps, from their data, and the
reasoning is recorded in the [wiki log](../wiki/log.md) (the two SLO
decision entries). In short:

- **Interactive** is judged on what a chat user feels: time to the first
  token, and the pace of the tokens after it. Both limits are user-facing
  budgets (a first token that reads as instant, and a token rate well above
  reading speed), not values fitted to the curve. They happen to fall on
  the baseline's knee, which leaves every experiment room to move the
  headline. A looser pair would have put the boundary between two levels
  with the same throughput, where no experiment could show a gain; a p99
  pair would rest on too few samples per level.
- **Throughput** is judged on completion time, because a batch
  summarisation user waits for the finished summary. TTFT and TPOT stay in
  the tables as diagnostics. A TTFT-plus-TPOT alternative put its boundary
  on a knife edge at a level with no throughput advantage.
- **Known weakness.** The first interactive level that fails the SLO fails
  its TPOT bound by only a few percent (the margin is in the headline table
  below). A small TPOT improvement would bring that level inside and move
  the headline by a large step, which would overstate a small effect. That
  is why every SLO cell carries its margin, and why each experiment's
  write-up quotes the margin, not only the pass or fail.

What the tables show:

- Output grows almost linearly with concurrency through the middle of the
  sweep, then stops: the top level adds almost nothing over the one below
  it while TPOT roughly doubles. That is the knee.
- Memory is not what limits it. KV-cache usage stays low, nothing waits,
  every sequence runs, and nothing is preempted. The GPU sits at its power
  limit from the middle of the sweep up, so decode is compute- or
  power-bound at these batch sizes. That is an inference from the power
  and utilisation telemetry, not a profile.
- Every level prefilled its prompts' unique part (engine check `ok`), so no
  level was served from an earlier level's cache.

What the tables show:

- Output peaks in the middle of the sweep and then drops at the next level,
  before any preemption. Why it drops is not answered by this data. The
  prefill budget (`max_num_batched_tokens` against long documents) and the
  power limit are the candidates; that is Experiment A's question.
- At the top of the sweep the KV pool is the limit, as the profile was
  sized to make it: KV usage is near full, fewer sequences run than the
  scheduler allows, requests wait, and TTFT becomes queueing time.
  Preemption happens but is small; queueing, not recomputation, dominates.
- The compliant peak is also the throughput peak, and the margins on both
  sides of the SLO boundary are wide.

## Experiment A: scheduler budgets (batching)

- **Baseline:** the tables above, at `--max-num-batched-tokens 2048` (vLLM's default on this card).
- **Hypothesis** (logged before the data, [wiki/log.md](../wiki/log.md)): at 2048 each engine step
  fits about one throughput-profile document beside the running decodes, so long prompts are
  admitted slowly and every decode step carries a large prefill chunk. A larger budget should
  admit documents faster, shrink the throughput dip past the peak, raise output tok/s from the
  peak upward, and raise TPOT (steps carrying more prefill take longer). The interactive
  profile's prefills are short, so it is the control: no material change expected.
- **Change:** `--max-num-batched-tokens 8192` (`deploy/compose/engine/a-mnbt8192.env`), the value
  vLLM itself uses on cards of 70 GB and up. Nothing else changes. The engine was started twice
  so the measured start had a warm compile cache.
- **Benchmark:** the levels around each baseline knee, same seed, prefix cache reset before every
  level (`scripts/sweep.sh` with `LEVELS_<PROFILE>`).
- **Result:** partly supported, and neither SLO boundary moves.
  - Throughput TTFT falls at the middle and upper levels: long prompts really are admitted
    faster. Output tok/s rises modestly past the peak.
  - The dip past the peak survives, smaller. So the prefill budget is only part of its cause.
  - TPOT did not rise as predicted.
  - The interactive control moves by amounts one run per config cannot separate from noise.
  - Max compliant goodput is essentially unchanged on both profiles: the first failing level
    still fails.
- **Trade-off:** the larger step budget reserves more activation memory, so the KV pool is
  smaller (engine facts below). At the top throughput level that shows up as more preemption and
  worse queueing TTFT. For this card and these SLOs the default budget stays.
